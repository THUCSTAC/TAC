# How to Give a Good Recitation (Exercises Session)

This section tries to answer:

- Why is a recitation worth giving?
- How to give a good recitation?

## Why is a recitation worth giving?

Let's first discuss why to have recitations, i.e. "what is a good recitation."

Let's list some reasons why there's no recitation now.

Is a recitation important for students? If many students don't even attend the teacher's lecture, would they come to a TA's recitation? Probably not. Students' time is tight.

For the TA, what's the benefit of trying a recitation? Just spending time preparing and teaching? Usually TAs already face many tasks — grading, tutoring, etc. Asking them to also run recitations seems a bit much. In fact, many teachers don't require TAs to run recitations.

## How to give a good recitation?

### Designing a recitation

- Clarify the positioning: is it reviewing/extending lecture content, or introducing assignment ideas? Clarify audience and goals.
- It helps if the TA also attends the teacher's lecture.
- Reference similar overseas courses' lecture/recitation content.
- Usually not many students come; then consider:
  - Designing fun interactive activities/questions, group discussions.
  - Live code demos (vscode extension storyteller for recording).
  - It helps if the TA attends the teacher's lecture.

### Before and after

- Before, ask some students about difficulties from lectures/assignments.
- After, briefly collect feedback: what they gained / how it was / what's still unclear. After each recitation, allow students to answer "any questions?" via a questionnaire — some won't raise their hand but will fill a form.

### Lecturing and interaction

- Practice clear Mandarin: proper pace, clear enunciation (practice tongue-twisters/voiceover/singing).
- Replay the recitation recording/sound, feeling what students hear.
- Watch whether students keep up, and try some interaction.

### One-line answers to common confusions

- How to handle "no students come to recitation but many have tutoring needs"? — Clarify audience (usually weaker students); match supply and demand; if students need one-on-one tutoring, do recitation first half and offline tutoring second half.
- How to design a recitation? — Review/extend lecture content / introduce assignment ideas; clarify audience and goals; vary and try more; ask how students feel.
